The Inevitable Demise of Classical RSA

Modern global commerce, military networks, and confidential personal messaging rely fundamentally on asymmetric public-key cryptography — notably RSA and Elliptic Curve Diffie-Hellman (ECDH). Their security rests upon the computational intractability of finding prime factors of large integers on classical Turing architectures.

However, Peter Shor's 1994 quantum algorithm proved that a sufficiently scaled, fault-tolerant quantum computer running quantum Fourier transforms can solve prime factorization in polynomial time $O((\log N)^3)$. Nation-state adversaries are currently executing "Harvest Now, Decrypt Later" (HNDL) data interceptions. Kavach provides the immediate remedy: drop-in hybrid cryptographic wrapping.
